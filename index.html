Timyassa — Fuerteventura Yoga Retreat, 12–19 October 2026

The retreat

A week at the edge of the Atlantic

Timyassa is Tim Sibley’s week on Fuerteventura: a small group, a private house, and yoga held at the three hinges of the day — sunrise, midday, sunset.

October on the island is still warm, the trade winds kind, the beaches long. The work is simple. Practise. Rest. Walk the volcanic shore. Let a week away do what a week away can do.

Flights and accommodation are included. The house is a coastal setting with room to practise, eat, and sit out the heat of the afternoon. Nothing about the week is hurried, and nothing is asked of you except to arrive.

Each day

Sunrise, midday, sunset

The distinctive shape of the week is the daily rhythm: practice on the beach at first light, again in the heat of the day, and once more as the sun goes down.

01

Sunrise Yoga

On the beach · first light

The first session of the day is held on the beach at sunrise. Cool sand, a long horizon, and a practice that wakes the body before the island grows loud.

02

Midday Yoga

At the house · the heat of the day

A second session during the day, in shade, with time to deepen practice and reconnect with the group before the afternoon opens out again.

03

Sunset Yoga

On the beach · last light

The final session is held on the beach at sunset: a slower close, the water going gold, and a memorable end to each day.

Fuerteventura

Sand, wind, volcanic quiet

The island is arid and generous: pale beaches, dark rock, a house with still water, and hours between practice that belong entirely to you. The photographs below suggest the atmosphere of the week, not a catalogue of named rooms or routes.

Previous retreats

What the week tends to leave behind

The reflections below are illustrative, written for this website to convey the tone of previous retreats. They are not verified reviews from named guests.

What I remember is the quiet of the beach at half past six — just our group, the water, and Tim talking us in as if we had all morning. The midday session in the shade of the house is where my practice actually changed: less performing, more listening. I flew home tired in the best way.

Claire Houghton Winchester · illustrative reflection

Tim does not fill the room with noise. He sets a pace and lets you meet it. Practising outdoors — wind, sand in the mat, the odd dog on the beach — made the week feel less precious and more true. By Wednesday we were eating together without performing friendliness. I still use the sunset sequence when I get in from work.

James Okonkwo Leeds · illustrative reflection

I was anxious about keeping up. Tim teaches to the room that is actually there. The group was mostly our age, which I had not expected to matter, and it did — nobody was filming themselves. After sunset we would sit and watch the light go. I came home sleeping through the night for the first time in months.

Eleanor Fraser Edinburgh · illustrative reflection

I go because my wife goes, which is an unpromising start. By day three I was looking forward to the sunrise walk down to the beach. Tim’s teaching is practical — where to put your hands, when to stop — and the rest of the day is allowed to be rest. Coffee on the terrace, a book, a swim. I would go again.

Martin Ellis Bristol · illustrative reflection

What I value is serious practice without the performance of seriousness. Sunrise on the sand is as good as it sounds; midday is where I work on the things I avoid at home; sunset is almost a conversation with the week. The group last time included people I would never choose on paper, and would now choose first. I came back feeling as if someone had opened a window.

Anita Desai London · illustrative reflection
